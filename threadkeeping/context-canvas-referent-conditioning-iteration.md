## Central posture

- Pronouns are natural collaboration language in conversation, quotations, authored voice, dialogue, and generated artifacts when collaborators can recover the reference.
- Artifact-bearing task inputs gain an explicit person, role, group, receiving function, or artifact anchor when a speaker-, addressee-, or group-relative term could change role, authority, audience, evidence, ownership, or personalization.
- A pronoun anchor names what a reference means in this task. Participant identity records retain their own governed system and purpose.
- A social proxy is a disclosed stand-in used to consider an absent role, population, audience, or future collaborator. Representation claims stay with people from the represented population and the evidence they authorize.
- Register names observable communication requirements. Named sources supply evidence, and named people retain decision authority.
- A prompt persona is a linguistic instruction. A persona vector is an activation-space construct measured in particular model experiments. The two remain adjacent and explicitly distinct.
- A benchmark is a bounded instrument for a stated reference or bias condition. The receiving collaboration tests whether the artifact works under its actual model, tools, history, roles, and decision boundary.
- Changes in benchmark or local-test behavior mean `LOOK HERE`: preserve the model/version, prompt, source field, configuration, affected workflow, and human receiver before interpreting the change.

## Source and claim boundaries

- RUFF shows that third-person pronoun fidelity can become brittle when another person enters a controlled narrative. It does not test whether deleting `me` from a lesson-plan request independently improves the lesson plan.
- AmbiCoref shows that evaluated coreference systems often failed to distinguish ambiguous from unambiguous minimal pairs. It does not establish that every current conversational model will miss the same ambiguity.
- LREC-COLING 2024 coreference evaluation shows substantial LLM coreference ability while finding that benchmark performance depends on prompting and evaluation method. Capability and benchmark result remain separate claims.
- Prompt-underspecification research supports proactive requirements discovery, evaluation, and monitoring while cautioning against exhaustive specification. The project inference is to begin with a compact set of decision-relevant fields and verify it locally.
- Persona-prompt research does not show a general factual-performance benefit from assigning social roles. Persona-vector research studies internal activation directions in named open models; it does not show that a personal pronoun creates a persona vector.
- Register and language variety can carry social cues. The public practice names an inference risk without treating every register choice as a demographic inference.
- The `Make me…` / named-role comparison is an inspectable design hypothesis. The second packet intentionally resolves several conditions, so it is not presented as a one-variable scientific experiment.
