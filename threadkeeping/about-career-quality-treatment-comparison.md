## Shared content contract

Every treatment must carry the same nine layers:

1. Scale and operating contexts.
2. Roles and organizational position.
3. Domains and business models.
4. Consequential operating situations.
5. Technical mechanisms and artifacts.
6. Changed work or outcome.
7. Human, quality, or applicability boundary.
8. Current-learning posture.
9. Common thread.

Every operating-situation record carries:

- `Situation`
- `Mechanism`
- `Changed work`
- `Boundary`

Career durations describe overlapping contexts and are not additive. Regulatory and quality references require product, jurisdiction, era, role, and applicability review before production publication.

## Cross-treatment invariants

- All five treatments render one shared content model.
- A field-complete coverage check verifies the title/deck context, facts, roles, situations, mechanisms, changed-work statements, boundaries, qualification states, common thread, and currentness notes after every treatment change.
- Static facts read as a definition ledger rather than a control bank.
- Career and Quality retain equal semantic standing.
- Claim, currentness, and applicability boundaries remain visible.
- Cobalt connects the system; powder identifies Career; sea-glass and Tiffany identify Quality; coral accompanies written consequential boundaries.
- Reflow precedes type reduction.
- Wide ledgers and blueprints use labeled local scrolling rather than page-level overflow.
- JavaScript-off rendering exposes the complete semantic source ledger.
- Print preserves both bodies of content.

## Evaluation criteria

- Content parity across treatments.
- Clear distinction between static facts and interactive controls.
- Executive scan value and practitioner depth.
- Career/Quality differentiation without hierarchy or ranking.
- Readability at 320, 390, 768, 1024, and 1440 pixels.
- Keyboard, no-JavaScript, print, forced-color, and reduced-motion behavior.
- Source qualification and currentness legibility.
- Production adaptation cost after selection.
