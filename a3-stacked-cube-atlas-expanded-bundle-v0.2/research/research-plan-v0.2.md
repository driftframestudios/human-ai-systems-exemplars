# Evidence-Guided Capability Transfer Atlas — Research Plan (v0.2)

**AS-OF:** 2026-07-24  
**Method authority:** Deep Research Atlas — Self-Contained Payload (v1.2)  
**Protected baseline:** Evidence-Guided Capability Transfer Model v0.1
**Target artifact:** one self-contained HTML document with inline SVG, CSS, JavaScript, evidence notes, and accessible text equivalents

## Research objective

Broaden the diagram without weakening its original semantic contract. The expanded artifact must show not only **where execution and decision authority resides**, but also the minimum evidence, runtime controls, data/accountability relationships, and legacy-retirement conditions that make a selective authority transfer credible in a regulated software setting.

## Protected claims

These remain unchanged unless direct evidence falsifies them:

1. A cube is a bounded software capability or service dependency, not a team, screen, or human activity.
2. Position encodes current execution or decision authority only.
3. Migration is a reversible routing and authority change through an explicit boundary, validation gate, and rollback route.
4. Visible gaps imply verified separability.
5. Human work remains a separate ribbon unless a bounded software capability is separately evidenced.
6. UNKNOWN remains visible and durable.
7. The four original authority frames remain the historical spine: current authority, boundary identified, shadow/dual-run, validated authority.

## Research questions

1. Which assurance obligations must be visible before a regulated capability can cross an authority gate?
2. Which runtime and data-flow relationships are semantically distinct enough to deserve typed connectors?
3. What evidence distinguishes a candidate boundary from an authority transfer?
4. Which signals support stabilization after cutover, and which additional evidence is needed before legacy retirement?
5. Which decisions remain explicitly human, legacy-authoritative, or unknown?
6. What provenance and confidence distinctions let a collaborator separate fact, corroborated inference, single-source claim, estimate, and unresolved question?
7. What is the strongest benign explanation for each apparent architectural gap, and what observation would change the conclusion?
8. How can the additions remain legible in one visual surface at wide and narrow widths?

## Planned evidence tranches

| Tranche | Role | Preferred sources |
|---|---|---|
| Existing capability-transfer record | Historical truth and protected semantics | Source record, canonical v0.1 registry, repository prototypes |
| Regulated-software assurance | Validation, traceability, records, security, human responsibility | Current FDA, eCFR, NIST, and W3C primary sources |
| Migration and runtime patterns | Routing, mirroring, compatibility, observability, rollback, retirement | First-party cloud/platform documentation, primary pattern descriptions, open specifications |
| Accessibility and provenance | Inspectable diagram behavior and evidence lineage | W3C standards and guidance |
| Disconfirmation | Limits, confounders, and alternative explanations | Contradictory or narrowing passages in the same primary sources; independent primary corroboration where necessary |

## Atlas evidence states

- **Verified fact:** directly supported by an authoritative primary source or the protected historical record.
- **Corroborated inference:** supported by at least two independent sources and explicitly marked as an inference.
- **Single-source claim:** useful but not sufficient to alter the canonical semantics.
- **Estimate:** method and confidence stated; never presented as observed fact.
- **Unresolved:** evidence absent, contradictory, inaccessible, or not specific enough.

## Confounder gates

Before treating a missing element as a meaningful diagram gap:

1. Check whether it is already implied by a protected semantic rather than absent.
2. Check whether the source describes a mandatory control, a contextual recommendation, or only one implementation option.
3. Check whether the recommendation is specific to medical-device software, production/quality systems, general software, or visualization accessibility.
4. Check whether the source speaks to pre-transfer validation, runtime monitoring, post-transfer stabilization, or final retirement.
5. Check whether the apparent gap is caused by the original diagram’s intentionally narrow authority lens.

## Candidate additions to test

These are hypotheses, not conclusions:

- orthogonal lenses for **authority**, **assurance**, **runtime/data flow**, and **evidence coverage**;
- an evidence stack linking requirement, risk/control, verification, trace, observation, decision, and rollback record;
- typed connectors for read, write, mirror, reconcile, authorize, observe, verify, route, rollback, and retire;
- explicit post-cutover stabilization and retirement-readiness conditions without rewriting the four historical frames;
- source-level provenance and confidence on every broadened element;
- visible human approval and residual-risk ownership;
- durable legacy and UNKNOWN states until their exit criteria are demonstrated.

## Decision rule

An addition enters the expanded diagram only when it:

1. answers one of the research questions;
2. is supported by a verified fact or corroborated inference;
3. does not overwrite the protected authority semantics;
4. can be encoded with a label, shape, line style, pattern, or position in addition to color;
5. has a clear falsifier or boundary condition;
6. remains comprehensible in an accessible table/text equivalent.

## Deliverable tests

- One HTML file; no external runtime, stylesheet, font, image, or script dependencies.
- Inline SVG is the primary visualization.
- All research-backed additions expose evidence state and source links.
- Original F0–F3 story and protected baseline remain inspectable.
- Wide and compact views remain usable at approximately 736 px and 320 px.
- Keyboard-operable native controls, visible focus, text equivalent, and reduced-motion support.
- No fabricated figures, probabilities, compliance claims, or source quotations.
- Coverage ledger records all attempted sources, depth, role, citations, and failures.
