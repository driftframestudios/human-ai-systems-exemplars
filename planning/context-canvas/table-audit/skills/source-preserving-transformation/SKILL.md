# Source-preserving transformation

## Contract

- **What:** A bounded transformation that separates original source, derived meaning, allowed edit spans and publication ownership.
- **When:** Authored markup, configuration or structured text must retain its surrounding form while a selected semantic projection changes.
- **Boundaries:** Extraction, authored-source patching and generator-owned output have distinct contracts. Current task authority governs mutation. Parser adoption, file publication and wider deployment remain separate decisions; parsed equivalence supplies its own claim alongside source preservation.
- **Result:** A source-bound edit plan or an explicit unsupported outcome, with admitted inputs, preserved intervals, fitting evidence and one receiving decision.

## Standing

Draft contract with bounded receiving exercises. Their observed behavior informs
revision; catalog promotion and broader adoption retain separate review.

## Fit and attention

- **Useful when:** Unowned formatting, comments, quoting, newlines or exact source bytes matter to an author or downstream receiver.
- **Poor fit when:** A canonical generator owns the whole output, or a read-only extractor only needs parsed meaning; those jobs can use their simpler output contracts.
- **Smallest useful dose:** One immutable source, one declared grammar and edit boundary, one guarded plan, and a result or recoverable disposition.
- **Foregrounds:** Ownership, offset units, pre-allocation budgets, full-asset admission and preservation outside edited spans.
- **May under-attend:** A source-preserving edit can still carry the wrong meaning; semantic, rendered and receiving-tool behavior need their own observations.
- **Material result:** A bounded source change whose intended meaning and retained source can be assessed independently.
- **Interactions:** Semantic design owns labels and value distinctions; cost analysis owns traversal and expansion claims; source-authority mapping owns projections; checkpointed campaigns can retain fitting observations.
- **Rest or release when:** The owned transformation and evidence are delivered, or a grammar, parser or publication boundary has a named receiver and concrete re-entry condition.

## Rules

### Rule: The output contract selects the representation

The receiving task first identifies extracted records, edits to authored source
or canonical generated output. Original source remains available alongside parsed
meaning when lexical identity matters. Normalization belongs to named semantic
fields; original values, qualifications and meaningful absence states retain
their source contract.

### Rule: Admission follows the selected owner

The grammar names supported structure, ambiguous or malformed input and the
boundary of opaque unselected content. Selection precedes semantic checks that
belong only to the selected owner. Whole-asset lexical and resource admission
still protects the parse boundary. A literal string resembling structure remains
content, and unsupported recovery receives an explicit disposition before edits.

### Rule: Offsets belong to one immutable source

Encoding, character or byte units, newline treatment and byte-order-mark handling
are explicit. Every span refers to the exact input from which it was derived.
Parser normalization or recovery supplies a parsed representation; an independent
outside-edit comparison establishes the preservation claim for that source.

### Rule: Stages have owned inputs and outcomes

Parsing, validation, semantic derivation, edit planning and application have
explicit boundaries. Named records expose their invariants and mutation owners;
an immutable wrapper retains a clear contract for its children. Validation covers
the complete asset and selected record shapes before publication. Pure
transformation decisions remain separable from filesystem or delivery adapters.

### Rule: Budgets precede expansion

Admission bounds the relevant source, token, nesting and output dimensions before
expensive work or repeated derived allocations. Repeated captions, escaped text
and copied suffixes contribute their actual work and allocation terms. A forward
cursor supports a traversal argument only when its copying and library costs are
also accounted for. Budget rejection preserves the input and identifies the
receiving limit.

### Rule: An edit plan carries its source identity

The plan binds to a digest or equivalent exact source identity, expected span
contents and ordered nonoverlap. Application checks the whole plan before
assembling the result. A source change reopens planning against the new revision;
an earlier plan retains its historical input rather than transferring authority
to similarly positioned content.

### Rule: Publication preserves the accepted asset boundary

A persisted file edit uses the authorized containment and link policy, required
metadata handling and supported atomic replacement mechanism. The complete
candidate and a changed-source guard precede replacement. Atomicity is per
asset unless the receiving system supplies a broader transaction. Concurrent
writers, interruption, durability and metadata beyond the supported contract
retain explicit limits and recovery owners.

### Rule: Recovery and implementation failures remain distinguishable

Typed domain outcomes identify unsupported input, exhausted budgets, stale plans
and recoverable asset failures. Independent valid assets may proceed within the
authorized batch, and the aggregate result retains every unresolved asset.
Unexpected implementation failures and interruptions stay visible to their
operator. A partially successful batch remains distinguishable from complete
acceptance.

### Rule: Evidence and parser adoption answer different questions

Meaning, idempotence where required, outside-edit preservation, admission and
publication each receive a fitting oracle. Bounded generated cases explore a
declared grammar; a discovered counterexample becomes a named regression.
Known-corpus parity retains its selected-owner coverage. A parser comparison
evaluates the complete required transformation, allocation and installation
burden; migration follows a separate adoption decision.

## Boundary scenarios

### Scenario: Quoted content resembles an owned attribute

- **Given:** Source contains attribute-like literal text, Unicode and mixed newlines.
- **When:** A selected label changes.
- **Then:** The literal content and outside-edit bytes retain their identity while a separate assertion checks the intended semantic label.

### Scenario: A generator owns the entire output

- **Given:** The receiving contract permits canonical serialization of a generated artifact.
- **When:** A representation is selected.
- **Then:** Canonical generation can satisfy that contract directly; authored-source span machinery rests unless a separate preservation need appears.

These scenarios specify decision branches. Local regression and corpus results
remain with their named evidence owners.

## Completion and re-entry

The transformation has a declared output and admission contract, guarded edits
or an explicit unsupported outcome, independent semantic and preservation
evidence, and a bounded publication result when publication was authorized.
Unresolved assets have a receiver and recovery condition. A new grammar case,
source drift, expansion counterexample or changed output ownership reopens the
smallest affected boundary; broader parser or delivery work keeps its own scope.
