# Evidence and structural balance

## Evidence stance

Use primary research and high-quality synthesis as companions to interpretation. Match each claim to the study’s design, sample, setting, and strength of inference. Distinguish association, mechanism, proposition, and causal warrant. Translate technical concepts into plain language while preserving their boundaries.

## Conceptual discipline

Psychological safety, belonging, social support, self-efficacy, collective efficacy, adaptive coping, sensemaking, feedback, compassion, positive emotion, and resilience remain related and distinct. The prose names the specific construct whose logic matters in the moment.

## Structural counterweights

Give analytic weight to workload, staffing, schedules, recovery, incentives, status, power, decision rights, access, unequal exposure, and institutional responsibility. Personal skill and relational support matter most clearly when the conditions that enable their use stay visible.

## Change literacy

Treat change as reciprocal sensemaking, supported experimentation, consequential voice, capability development, feedback, and institutionalization. Readiness includes beliefs about need, appropriateness, efficacy, and support. Progress becomes credible when participants can connect it to lived consequences.

## Cognitive psychology literacy

Use attention, appraisal, cognitive reappraisal, self-efficacy, collective efficacy, broaden-and-build dynamics, conservation of resources, identity, prosocial action, the progress principle, moral injury, and learning as context-sensitive lenses. Human cognition appears as situated within relationships and material conditions.

## Verification questions

- Does every claim match its evidence warrant?
- Do relationships and structures share explanatory weight with personal capacity?
- Are nearby concepts distinguished clearly?
- Does research deepen practical understanding in the same paragraph or section?
