# Small-Business and Entrepreneurial Leadership — Overview

Small enterprises live close to consequence. A pricing error reaches payroll; a supplier delay reaches a customer; a founder’s exhaustion reaches the whole team. That proximity can produce remarkable responsiveness, but it can also concentrate uncertainty, authority, and emotional load in a few people. Research on entrepreneurial action, bricolage, effectuation, self-efficacy, networks, psychological safety, business failure, and well-being offers a richer account than the familiar tale of the tireless founder. It shows resourcefulness as a social and cognitive practice while keeping scarcity, unequal access, and institutional conditions in view.

The first essay develops an integrative approach to acting under uncertainty: begin with available means, protect downside, learn through bounded commitments, and make information travel. The second challenges the romantic treatment of endurance, asking when adaptability merely subsidizes an unsustainable business model or an unequal environment. The third considers stewardship over time—the patient work of preserving purpose while revising methods, roles, and even the meaning of success. Together, the essays distinguish confidence from certainty, persistence from fixation, and resilience from solitary strain. They locate practical hope in dependable relationships, candid communication, inclusive decisions, and next steps small enough to test yet significant enough to teach.
