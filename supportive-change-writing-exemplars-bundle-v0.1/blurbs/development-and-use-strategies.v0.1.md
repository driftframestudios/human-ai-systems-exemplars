# Blurb development and use strategies

## Build by moment of use

Develop small sets for event pages, speaker introductions, post-event follow-up, learning catalogs, leadership programs, team-change communications, and donor or partner conversations. Each set can carry the same central qualities while foregrounding the situation most relevant to its readers.

## Build by credible voice

Expand the voice library across individual contributors, people managers, executives, learning leaders, accessibility advocates, service professionals, business owners, and adults returning to work. Preserve the vocabulary and specificity that make each role recognizable.

## Build by practical consequence

Create variants around one observable application: entering a new team, repairing a handoff, navigating a reorganization, asking for support, adapting communication, widening participation, recovering after pressure, or recognizing incremental progress.

## Build by evidence-aware resonance

Use light conceptual echoes—psychological safety, reciprocal sensemaking, self-efficacy, cognitive reappraisal, prosocial action, the progress principle, resource conservation, and recovery—where the audience benefits from them. Translate each concept into ordinary workplace language within the same statement.

## Preserve testimonial integrity

Published testimony carries a real participant’s reviewed experience, exact approved wording, and chosen attribution. Draft exemplars serve as prompts for reflection and revision. A participant can select a starting point, replace general language with lived specifics, and approve the final public form.

## Curate a balanced public set

Pair emotional warmth with practical detail. Mix immediate workplace usefulness, enduring supporter perspective, evidence-aware confidence, inclusion, managerial application, and event impact. A balanced set helps prospective audiences recognize both felt experience and concrete value.

## Connect short and long forms

Use a short statement as the public entry point and a longer approved statement where context strengthens credibility. Link deeper branches to essays when an audience wants comparative literacy, research context, or a fuller view of structural conditions.

## Review through observable signals

- The voice sounds specific to a real role and situation.
- The statement names a memorable idea or concrete consequence.
- Warmth and realism coexist.
- Participant ownership and approval remain visible.
- The public set represents several audience relationships and use moments.
