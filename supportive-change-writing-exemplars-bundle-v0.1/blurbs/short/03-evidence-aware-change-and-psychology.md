# Branch C — Evidence-aware change and psychology

### Draft 01

> The five steps quietly reflect what strong change practice looks like: create safety, work with existing capacity, communicate clearly, support people actively, and mark progress. Piglet’s story makes those principles human and memorable in warm, everyday language.

### Draft 02

> I appreciated the balance of warmth and substance. The framework connects naturally with psychological safety, adaptive coping, and the motivating power of small wins, and the whole experience feels conversational and useful.

### Draft 03

> Strategic Mapping gave me a useful way to think about self-efficacy during uncertainty: start with the skills and routines you can still access, then expand from there. That is a grounded, compassionate way to build confidence during personal and organizational change.

### Draft 04

> The message about joyful resilience felt grounded and credible: adapt the plan, lean on your people, notice small wins, and keep the hard parts fully visible. That balance of realism and hope made the session memorable and impactful.

### Draft 05

> Anyone familiar with Kotter’s change work will hear echoes of coalition-building and short-term wins, while the talk still feels personal and human. Piglet gives those ideas a human scale: find your pack, take the next workable step, and let small progress restore momentum.
