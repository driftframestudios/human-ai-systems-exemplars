A reusable decision hierarchy for making the area navigable before its first exemplar lands

Snag

Read

The collaboration needs layered service visibility

AI, agents, data stores, governance, review, and human work are service layers that shape the experience, not downstream details.

Move

Build a stage-by-layer matrix

Start with the visible journey, add support lanes, mark known, assumed, needs-to-surface, disputed, and ready-to-route cells, then name each handoff.

Cure

The whole topology can be inspected and routed

Transitions become behavior examples; data and system dependencies become architecture views; service outcomes become measurement candidates.

Guidance

Ask which support layer makes each visible step possible

Place AI where source, review, data, and escalation boundaries are visible, and keep governance and evidence inside the topology from the start.

Prototype connection — map roles and interactions before polishing the exemplar

Use the blueprint as-is when stages and actors are known. Invert it when they are not: an empty support cell, unnamed handoff, or unplaced review boundary becomes a research question and a reason to delay visual certainty.

Industry adaptation

Change the service journey, retain the layers

Stages, obligations, and participants vary; visible action, backstage work, AI, system, data, governance, evidence, and handoff lanes preserve the comparison.

Organizational definitions

Name local actors, statuses, and lines of authority

Teams can define their review, consent, audit, escalation, and ownership markers while every receiving actor retains a navigable route.

Pipeline and harness subset

Route only the stable parts downstream

Accepted transitions can become example cases; data and system cells can become implementation attachments; review boundaries can become evaluation and release checks.
