The Queue That Was Secretly a Status Machine

At-a-Glance

Within Foundation Medicine R&D, I combined principal-level product design with full-stack implementation to keep case assignment, evidence review, and sign-off moving while one capability family transferred into a new platform and consequential legacy areas retained named ownership.

Situation and stakes
A researcher-built GWT viewer had become a consequential clinical-genomics application. Case assignment, evidence review, and sign-off had to keep moving while the replacement took shape.
Scope and role
From an early Q1 2019 search redesign through the Q3 2022–Q1 2025 platform program, I worked as Principal Product Designer and Software Engineer III in a core seven-person delivery group within a 50-plus-engineer Computational Biology organization. Scientists, researchers, directors, executives, Cloud Operations, and Platform Engineering shaped the work.
Core constraint
Authentication identified a person; authorization still had to account for assay, product, geography, jurisdiction, qualification, and action. The visible queue encoded those distinctions together with operational state and tacit workflow inside a monolith.
Key decision
Model profiles, permissions, cases, and status explicitly; place evidence at the decision; turn participant language into testable scenarios; translate the Masonry concept into bounded typed components; and move capability through a recoverable migration.
Outcome and evidence
I designed and implemented new interfaces and views for specimen overview, variant detail, quality control, profiles, dashboards, search, notes, and shared components. Complex transformations, DTOs, database mediation, plugins, and portions of the underlying capability remained legacy-owned.
Relevance now
My profile and permission work gave qualified people the right work and supported later automatic assignment. My structured-note work also made human interventions countable across candidates for tiered automation.

Basis and limits: I designed and implemented the delivered interfaces and views within a partial migration; complex transformations, DTOs, database mediation, plugins, and portions of the underlying capability remained legacy-owned. Costing requires current inputs, technology choices require current constraints, and AI-assisted delivery effects require separate measurement.

Solution and implementation quick scan

Solution Architecture

  1. Debrief. I joined a research-built genomic viewer that had become clinical infrastructure, redesigned its search, and worked inside the core team to make curation, pathology, scientific, and engineering decisions visible in one operating experience.
  2. Bottleneck. Manual assignment, plate-wide failures, service limits, study-name conventions, SQL, Slack, email, and a macro-based tracker distributed work state across people and tools; broad authentication still needed assay-, jurisdiction-, profile-, and action-level rules.
  3. Intervention. I made profile, permission, assignment, case state, and recovery visible in the interface, moved bounded operational layers through a gradual migration, and translated participant language into testable scenarios through Specification by Example.
  4. Outcome. Profile and permission work eliminated recurring assignment errors and supported qualified automatic assignment; structured notes and visible operating state made interventions countable while clinical effect and causal magnitude remained separate measurement questions.
  5. Repeatability. Role-local context, Impact Mapping, profile-first decomposition, scenario specifications, and visible state continued to guide the work as screens, services, and organizational conditions changed.

Implementation Architecture

  1. Debrief. I used React, TypeScript, MUI, and Storybook to open a familiar contribution path into a GWT and Java estate, building the visual system, core tables, forms, operating modes, and shared component library.
  2. Bottleneck. GWT-to-React interop concentrated lifecycle, validation, DTO, payload, and rendering risk; I retired a widely liked global Masonry treatment when its coupling obstructed a stable capability transfer.
  3. Intervention. The exploding table, permission ledger, floating drawer, record-local collaboration, Case view, and Automated Advice put evidence, action, authorization, and state at the point of judgment while server checks retained access authority.
  4. Outcome. A shared dashboard replaced a daily SQL-and-email reconstruction that exceeded thirty minutes, service reads and structured transmission fields replaced tracker jobs, and intervention counts exposed candidates for tiered automation.
  5. Repeatability. Unit-Test-First, the Upstream Revision Loop, Storybook, and Testing Library connected scenarios, tests, components, and revisions so operating knowledge could survive team and platform change.

The Boolean Building Blocks That Were Quietly an ETL Substrate

At-a-Glance

At Foundation Medicine, I worked directly with directors and executives across scientific, business, and medical-affairs functions; biomarker and biometrics specialists; production engineering; and biomarker-repository operations. As Senior Manager and Principal Designer, then Product Designer and Software Engineer III, I helped six working groups turn their knowledge into one inspectable clinical-trial rule grammar; the shipped coordination interface later became the foundation for a repeatable transformation pathway.

Situation and stakes
Foundation Medicine’s genomic-profiling products inform cancer-care and clinical-trial decisions. Six groups needed one usable way to translate partner data, biomarker criteria, cohort logic, implementation state, and regulatory consequence while retaining their distinct knowledge.
Scope and role
From 2019–2023, I worked directly with directors, opened the engagement, negotiated time with specialists carrying live work, and coordinated decisions across business units—first as Senior Manager and Principal Product Designer on Data Products, then as Product Designer and Software Engineer III in Computational Biology Engineering.
Core constraint
Each population held a different part of the rule system. The product needed one shared, inspectable model that retained partner-specific meaning and clarified the handoffs.
Key decision
Define a shared object grammar with nested Boolean rules, deploy it in tools people already used, and let partner data seed a reviewable initial state that people could correct.
Outcome and evidence
I designed the shared rule grammar and complete interaction states; engineering built the application in one quarter while the design remained stable. Each Data Manager could set up three to five more cases per week, and measurable setup errors fell 50%.
Relevance now
The case connects participatory specification with executable data contracts: shared visual objects can carry meaning from inquiry into templates, mappings, and human-reviewed automation.

Basis and limits: Production use established the throughput gain, measurable error reduction, and durable grammar. My 2023 work established a partner-fed proposal method; its numeric result remains undisclosed, and the partner-facing extension reached design and partial implementation.

Solution and implementation quick scan

  4. Outcome. Engineering built the stable design in one quarter; Data Managers set up three to five more cases each week with a measured 50% error reduction and about ten minutes saved per setup, while Account Managers began defining trial standards directly with biopharma partners.

  4. Outcome. Participants contributed through familiar tools, other teams reused the shapes, and the production team inherited explicit states, dispatches, error paths, and modular components; the later partner-facing extension reached design and partial implementation.
  5. Repeatability. A viable transfer starts from local access, policy, regulated-record, browser, and platform conditions, then places reusable research components inside tools people already use while keeping state and partner boundaries explicit.
