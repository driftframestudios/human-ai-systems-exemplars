# Prototype-assisted decision probe

## What

A bounded use of a real instrument that can earn or release its place in a collaboration through an observed receiving result.

## When

A specific prototype affordance could change a decision, expose an assumption, route evidence or improve a handoff.

## Boundaries

The instrument's schema and source own its meaning. Scores, weights, probabilities, working drafts and accepted reviews remain distinct. A successful import/export proves that path, not the instrument's efficacy. Direct reasoning is the comparison route.

## Result

A pre/post decision comparison, reusable working output where useful, and a retain, revise, defer or abandon disposition with a concrete next observation.

## Rules

1. Record the current decision, uncertainty and intended receiver before using the instrument.
2. Consider the available suite by reader job. Select one affordance rather than filling every available prototype.
3. Read its current schema and output contract; use native interaction or a declared programmatic boundary. Preserve working/reviewed distinctions and source provenance.
4. Capture what changed: choice, assumption, boundary, next observation or receiving handoff. Unchanged decisions remain a legitimate neutral result.
5. Record setup, harness and interpretation costs separately from prototype behavior. Use comparable opportunities and acknowledge learning-order effects.
6. Bound an initial epoch by both decision opportunities and a stop event. Continue only for a named transfer uncertainty; release repeated use that adds the same structure.
7. Preserve useful code, fixtures or method findings with their actual artifact owner even when the instrument is unsuitable for the original task.
8. Promote a portable skill or public exemplar after independent review and a demonstrated receiving use. One successful demonstration leaves general efficacy open.

## Completion

The collaborator knows whether the instrument changed this decision, what evidence supports that judgment, and whether another distinct opportunity is worth the effort.
