# Agency and action writing

## Contract

- **What:** A sentence-level editorial pass that restores the actor, actual action, object, and material consequence when prose hides one or more of them.
- **When:** Public narratives, case studies, summaries, table labels, or instructions use passive construction, entry metaphors, self-reference, nominalization, or a generic abstraction where the actual work would communicate more clearly.
- **Boundaries:** Voice remains flexible. Accurate collective, system, unknown-actor, and deliberately impersonal constructions remain available. Quotations, source titles, governed terms, claim strength, contribution boundaries, and privacy constraints retain their meaning.
- **Result:** A reader can identify what happened, who or what made it happen, what changed, and why it mattered without being pulled into forced candor or confessional narration.

## Fit and attention

- **Useful when:** A sentence says that work “was undertaken,” that someone “entered through” a topic, that an account “reports” its own author’s work, or that a generic “surface” changed while leaving the interface, workflow, page, model, or decision boundary unnamed.
- **Poor fit when:** The existing impersonal construction already makes the supported action, object, and consequence clear while unknown or protected agency remains appropriately unspecified; the passive construction correctly foregrounds an affected person or object; a technical abstraction names a real boundary; or the requested artifact requires exact source language.
- **Smallest useful dose:** Revise the smallest sentence, label, or paragraph that obscures the action; preserve surrounding voice that already works.
- **Interactions:** `supportive-change-writing` owns the artifact’s broader register, evidence calibration, and change posture. This skill supplies a focused agency-and-action pass. Semantic Attractor Design keeps the intended construction prominent, while `contextual-jargon-scan` can assess an abstraction whose audience fit remains uncertain.
- **Rest or release when:** A single reading reveals the actor, action, object, consequence, and truthful contribution boundary, or establishes why an impersonal or abstract form is more accurate.

## Rules

### Rule: Actual work carries the sentence

The revision names the action a person, team, system, or process performed. Specific verbs such as interviewed, mapped, redesigned, negotiated, configured, tested, reviewed, migrated, or approved replace spatial metaphors and nominalizations when the source supports them.

### Rule: Agency matches the contribution

First-person language appears when authorship or responsibility matters and the evidence supports it. Team, role, and system contributions remain distinct. A direct rewrite never enlarges authority, invents an actor, or turns participation into ownership.

### Rule: Direct voice preserves professional distance

Direct writing states relevant choices and actions. It does not require personal disclosure, emotional confession, motive claims, or a performance of candor. The artifact’s audience and purpose continue to govern first person, third person, and collective voice.

### Rule: Concrete nouns precede earned abstractions

A page, interface, workflow, queue, service, contract, model, or decision is named when that noun carries the useful meaning. An abstraction such as *surface* remains welcome when it identifies a genuine boundary, receiving plane, or joined set of information and action. When needed, its first use includes the concrete referent.

### Rule: Terms receive contextual judgment

Search terms can locate candidates for review; they do not decide the revision. No word becomes a repository-wide defect by frequency alone. Each occurrence is retained, clarified, or replaced according to its function in the sentence and the reader’s task.

### Rule: Consequence completes the claim

The sentence or nearby passage connects action with an observable effect, decision, capability, or changed condition when the source establishes one. Evidence class and uncertainty remain intact.

### Rule: Missing facts remain visible

When the source does not establish an actor, actual action, concrete object, or observable consequence, the revision changes only what the evidence supports. An impersonal construction can preserve the known state. Working notes name the missing fact as a question for the source owner; published prose leaves it unspecified rather than filling the gap with a plausible invention.

## Operating sequence

1. Read the whole claim and identify its audience, evidence class, and contribution boundary.
2. Mark the actor, actual action, object, and consequence already established by the source.
3. Identify the element hidden by passive construction, metaphor, self-reference, nominalization, or a generic container term.
4. Rewrite the smallest unit with the most accurate actor and verb. Name a concrete artifact or activity where it improves recognition.
5. Retain an abstraction when it adds real boundary meaning; pair it with a concrete referent when the audience may need orientation.
6. When a needed fact is unavailable, produce the smallest faithful revision and route the missing actor, action, object, or consequence back as a question.
7. Read the revision for varied cadence, professional distance, shared agency, claim strength, and fit with the surrounding artifact.

## Completion

The pass is complete when the prose says what happened at the useful level of specificity, assigns agency and consequence truthfully, preserves the artifact’s chosen voice, and retains every abstraction that contributes meaning. Candidate words remain matters for contextual review rather than prohibition.
